Slide text
Growing up with hearing loss
Most significant consequence of growing up with hearing loss is the difficulty in perceiving speech
This limitation has a direct effect on the ability to develop one’s own words and subsequent language
When language development is delayed, there is a cascading effect on many aspects of a child’s psychosocial development

Counseling · revised slide 3

Slide text
Delayed language development due to hearing loss can effect a child’s psychosocial development:

Self concept
Emotional development
Family concerns
Social competence

Counseling · revised slide 4

Slide text
Self Concept
Individuals are not born with self concept intact 
Self concept is learned by absorbing input, feedback and reactions from those around us
Children internalize these reactions and allow others’ attitudes to define themselves to themselves (“I see myself the way you tell me to see me”)
Children with hearing loss are at risk for developing a negative self concept, likely due to negative reactions regarding their hearing loss

Counseling · revised slide 5

Slide text
Self Concept continued…
“Hearing aid effect”
Psychosocial reaction to the presence of a hearing aid. The viewer has negative assumptions about the wearer
If appearance of hearing aids causes negative reactions in others, their reactions is likely perceived by the perosn wearing the hearing aids

Counseling · revised slide 6

Slide text
Emotional Development
We use language to describe, interpret and understand the abstract nature of emotions
Language deficitlimited experience with emotional languagedelayed awareness and understanding of own emotions and emotions of others
Children with hearing loss miss overhearing adults talk about managing their emotions
Children with hearing loss less accurate in identifying others’ emotions than children without hearing loss

Counseling · revised slide 7

Slide text
Emotional Development continued…
Children with hearing loss have poor understanding of affective words
Affective vocabulary: words/phrases that describe feelings
Without intervention, deficits in one area of emotional development have cascading effect on other areas:
	child can’t express feelingsunable to understand 	feelingsdifficulty with empathyempathy is a prerequisite for friendship development

Counseling · revised slide 8

Slide text
Family Concerns
Most children with hearing loss are born to hearing parents
Most parents experience emotional reactions consistent with the “grief cycle”
Parents do not move through the stages of the grief and recover. Rather, they move back and forth through these stages with different stages of their child’s development.

Counseling · revised slide 9

Slide text
Family concerns continued
Professionals cannot expect parents to be “over their grief by now”
Parents must let go of being the parents of a “perfect child” so they can accept their new reality of being parents of a child with hearing loss
Clinician needs to understand that the issues emphasized may not be at the forefront of parents’ concerns. Need to “speak their language”.

Counseling · revised slide 10

Slide text
Social competence
Limited communication results in poor social competence
Difficulty with:
Capacity to think independently
Capacity for self direction and self control
Understanding the feelings, motivations and needs of self and others
Flexibility
Ability to tolerate frustration
Ability to rely on and be relied on by others
Maintaining healthy relationships with others

Counseling · revised slide 11

Slide text
The Teen Years
Turbulent time of increased self consciousness and uncertainty
Hearing loss can increase teen struggles for self-awareness and self-expression
Mainstreamed teens with hearing loss often struggle to find identity in a vacuum. 
Desire to conform can include a desire to reject amplification-based hearing aids
Students need to develop appropriate social and interpersonal skills so they can advocate for their needs now and in the future

About being deaf
The more severe the hearing loss, the more severe the psychosocial and emotional problems 
Deaf children and adults tend to be characterized as :
Impulsive
Compulsive
Egocentric
Rigid
Anxious 

These characteristics are not caused by the hearing loss, but rather by the communication problems that result from the hearing loss

Grief:
Feelings need to be acknowledged and accepted, not judged
Feeling are neither good, nor bad, they just are
It is always a mistake, no matter how nicely we put it, to tell a person that he should not feel a particular way.  This invalidates the person’s feelings.

Slide text
Feelings Involved with Grief
Shock/Denial/Numbness - protects parents from emotional overload and allows parents to build up the energy needed for the work ahead of them
Anger -  Parents feel cheated.  Anger hides their fear.  Many professionals are very afraid of the anger and respond defensively.  Need to help parents capture and direct their energy effectively.

Client-Centered or Family-Centered Counseling
A communication disorder always exists within a “family” context.  The focus must always be in some way on the communicative disorder and how it is affecting the client and family’s coping abilities.

Counseling · revised slide 31

Slide text
What are the benefits of Family-Centered Care?(not specific to hearing impairment)
improved clinical decision making that is based on better information
a greater understanding of the individual’s and family’s strengths and needs
better follow-through when the treatment plan is developed collaboratively
more effective communication leading to fewer misunderstandings  
better outcomes and greater satisfaction with services 
American Academy of Pediatrics, 2012

Counseling · revised slide 35

How to Deal With Resistance:
Maintain unconditional positive regard
Listen actively
Give the person time to blow off steam
If you have to interrupt, do it with dignity
Check your response to resistance and maintain your poise
Pause briefly, then respond to what was said
Help the person feel understood and validated
