Start with the question
Assessment is a clinical argument. Week one puts clinical reasoning at the center: gather information from several sources, interpret it critically, and decide what the evidence supports. The seven-stage framework begins with referral and review. History and interview come next, then observation, assessment, analysis, diagnosis and recommendations, and the report and results conference. A test score is evidence within that process. It cannot replace the process.

The four integrity features
The slide you flagged for Test One names four features. Be thorough. Use a variety of assessment methods. Use evidence-based approaches. Tailor the assessment to the individual. Keep their explanations paired correctly: valid and reliable methods belong to evidence based; age, skill level and ethnocultural background belong to individual tailoring.

Three components; four steps
Evidence-based practice integrates research and client data, clinical expertise, and the client or caregiver perspective. Internal evidence comes from your client; external evidence comes from scientific research. The four steps are frame the question, gather evidence, assess the evidence, and make the clinical decision. Assessment must also remain within your professional competence.

Match the tool to its purpose
The assigned videos extend this reasoning to test selection. Check purpose, diagnostic accuracy, normative representation, relevant skills, reliability, and sufficiency. A screening instrument does not become a diagnostic instrument because it is convenient. Sensitivity and specificity apply to the particular target disorder studied. If a test score conflicts with everyday communication, gather converging evidence and document the limits.
