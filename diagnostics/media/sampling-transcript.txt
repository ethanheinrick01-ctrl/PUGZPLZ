Speech sampling, rate & intelligibility

Administer DDK consistently
For the course’s more common DDK method, measure the seconds needed for a specified number of repetitions. Instruct and model, say go and start timing, then stop after twenty repetitions of a single syllable. After examining syllables separately, time ten repetitions of puh tuh kuh. Record the conditions and findings. The alternate method counts syllables produced within a fixed time.

Make the sample representative
The current Week four slide specifies fifty to one hundred minimum utterances. Build rapport, minimize interruptions, wait, and use topics that elicit more than names or yes-no answers. Vary activities, contexts and partners when possible. Pictures with actions, personal events and narrative retelling provide connected language. Reading supplies fixed words that cannot be avoided, but is appropriate only for a client who can read the material.

Work the rate
Record at least one to two minutes of connected speech for the course rate procedure. Time the sample in seconds, count its words, divide words by seconds and multiply by sixty. Sixty words in seventy-five seconds gives forty-eight words per minute. The clinical question is how rate affects this person’s communication. A norm alone cannot describe that effect.

Intelligibility depends on context
Intelligibility is how well a listener understands speech. Error type and frequency matter, but so do listener familiarity, context, face-to-face contact, nonverbal cues and environment. Transcribe a representative sample and calculate words understood divided by total words, times one hundred. Record the conditions. Eighty understood words out of one hundred is eighty percent intelligibility, not an articulation percentile rank.
