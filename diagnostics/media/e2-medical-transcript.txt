From condition to assessment question

A medical diagnosis helps you decide what to investigate. It does not tell you everything a person can do. Chapter five connects medical and psychiatric conditions with possible communication and swallowing concerns. Keep the word possible attached to those associations. A syndrome is a characteristic set of symptoms. The individual assessment establishes which features are actually present and how they affect function.

Start by following the relevant pathway. Several conditions in the deck can affect hearing, and hearing difficulties can accompany speech and language concerns. The slides place otitis media and mastoiditis in that pathway. Craniofacial conditions can direct attention toward resonance, speech, feeding, and swallowing. For example, the deck connects Pierre Robin sequence with feeding concerns, possible hearing related language delays, and resonance issues associated with cleft palate. The condition guides the questions. It does not replace hearing evidence or direct examination.

Neurological conditions can affect several systems at once. Stroke may involve language, speech motor control, cognition, and swallowing. Parkinson's disease is associated in the deck with dysarthria, swallowing difficulty, and cognitive impairment. Motor neuron disease can affect speech and swallowing. Developmental and learning conditions can shift the focus toward language learning, social communication, and functional participation. Similar concerns appear across different diagnoses, so one symptom does not identify its cause.

Turn each association into an assessment question. If hearing is a concern, what hearing information is available? If speech sounds different, is the issue articulation, resonance, voice, or a broader motor speech pattern? If eating or drinking is difficult, what swallowing assessment or referral is indicated? Combine the history with relevant direct evidence and information from the team. Use the condition directory to retrieve the specific associations from the slides, then return to the person in front of you. The medical label points toward evidence you need. It does not supply the answer in advance.
