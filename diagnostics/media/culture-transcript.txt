Difference or disorder
The primary assessment decision is difference or disorder. A pattern unlike Mainstream American English is not automatically a disorder. Learn the communication expectations of the client’s community, languages and dialects. Limited eye contact, for example, may reflect a cultural expectation. Interpret the observation before assigning a clinical label.

ASKED is an ongoing process
The cultural competence model is ASKED: awareness, skill, knowledge, encounters and desire. Competence requires continuing self-assessment. A language history asks which languages are used, how frequently, where and with whom. Assess abilities in both languages, one language at a time, with appropriate assistance when needed. Family, teachers and interpreters contribute information that a single English test cannot supply.

Teach, then examine learning
Dynamic assessment compares an independent attempt with performance after instruction and support. Observe what cues help, how much support is needed, and whether learning transfers. Improvement can support an experience-based explanation. Limited improvement raises concern, but the whole profile still matters. Dynamic evidence is not simply a second administration of an unchanged test.

Use interpreters deliberately
Brief the interpreter on the purpose and tasks, work together during the assessment, and debrief afterward. Speak to the client and check understanding. Translating an English standardized test does not create validated norms for another language. Modified tasks can provide useful descriptive information; identify those limits in the report instead of presenting an invalid normative comparison.
