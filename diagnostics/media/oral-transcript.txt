Structure and function
The oral mechanism examination asks whether structure or function contributes to communication. Observe posture and natural rest before giving commands. Then compare movement, symmetry, range, coordination and endurance. Standard precautions apply across settings. Gloves, adequate lighting and appropriate examination tools support clear observation. Record what you see and how it affects the task.

Choose tasks by purpose
Pucker and smile examine lip range and symmetry. Puffing and holding cheek air probes lip closure and pressure, with attention to nasal emission. Tongue movements examine excursion, range, symmetry and strength. Sustaining ah allows observation of velar movement and the uvula, alongside nasality. The point of Form six dash one is to understand why the task is used, not recite commands without a purpose.

Interpret the pattern
Stability supports a position. Mobility moves through range. Dissociation moves one part independently. Grading controls speed, force and range. A jaw moving during a tongue task may be compensation rather than independent tongue control. The assigned video distinguishes horizontal overjet from vertical overbite. Its submucous cleft warning combines a bifid uvula, translucent midline zone and posterior hard-palate notch. One sign alone does not establish the diagnosis.

DDK gives more than speed
Alternating motion rate repeats one syllable; sequential motion rate changes syllables, such as puh tuh kuh. Observe accuracy, rhythm, consistency and coordination as well as rate. Adequate single-syllable repetitions with difficulty sequencing raises a motor-planning question; it is not a stand-alone CAS diagnosis. Relate each observation to speech, resonance, feeding or airway function and the need for additional assessment.
