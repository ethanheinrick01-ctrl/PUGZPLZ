Current COMD 4756 slides, assignments, transcripts and supplied source materials govern the facts and keys. Gonsoulin’s Professor DNA and codex from earlier Language Disorders exams govern question construction. Historical response patterns do not establish Diagnostics topic probabilities or the exact exam draw.

## Original pool

The Exam focus objective pool contains 180 new questions: 90 MC and 90 T/F. Each of the nine chapters supplies ten of each format. That equal supply supports reusable practice; it is not a claim about chapter weighting on the exam.

| Observed construction habit | Application in the new pool |
|---|---|
| Neighboring concepts and familiar but misplaced choices | Parallel alternatives distinguish assessment stages, types of evidence, movement capacities, scoring rules and task purposes. |
| Precise qualifiers and context anchors | A task, language history, support condition or stated criterion determines the defensible conclusion. |
| NOT, EXCEPT and LEAST distinctions | Meaningful negative items ask for the exception within one clinical or conceptual neighborhood. No historical percentage is enforced as a quota. |
| Sibling terms, direction and sequence swaps in T/F | Statements change the construct, stage, denominator or inference boundary. False statements do not rely on an “always means false” trick. |
| Applied sequences | Cases ask which evidence or next assessment step addresses the stated unresolved question. |
| Specific numerical reasoning | Original raw-score, age, scale, rate and intelligibility examples supply the conventions and denominators needed for one defensible answer. |

Stems and choices do not ask what the lecturer said or what appeared on a slide. Feedback gives the key, a substantive explanation, each MC distractor rationale and exact source locators. Choice order is shuffled without forcing an answer-letter balance. Earlier saved activities retain their original order.

The pool receives independent keyless solving, source review, a near-duplicate review and checks for answer-length, punctuation, grammar and detail cues. Mean option lengths are only a screening measure; reviewers also inspect whether the alternatives present meaningful competing interpretations.
